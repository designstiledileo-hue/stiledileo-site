# Inquiry and Estimate Templates

Prepared 2026-10-23. Owner-reviewed drafts, not sent messages or promises of price, availability or technical suitability. Replace bracketed fields with real project information before use. These brackets are internal drafting fields, not website placeholders.

## Working method

Use the existing private lead register. Keep one lead ID per actual project inquiry. First check the information already supplied, then ask only for what is missing. Do not make a customer repeat a form they already completed.

Review inquiries at practical points during the working day and give each one a next action/date. No public response-time guarantee is introduced. A site visit is not the default for an unreviewed or unsupported job.

## 1. New homeowner inquiry with missing information

Hi [first name], thank you for contacting Stile di Leo about your [fireplace/wall]. Could you send a photo of the full surface, your city and approximate width and height? If you have an example of the look you like, include that too. You do not need to know the material name. I will review the information before recommending a next step.

Vladyslav

Link if helpful: https://stiledileo.com/#estimate. That form accepts one required surface image, phone or email, optional city and project note. Suggest an inspiration URL in the notes or a separate email for additional images; do not promise multiple uploads.

## 2. Photos received; potentially suitable project

Hi [first name], thank you for the photos and details. I can see [specific visible observation, without guessing the concealed substrate]. To assess the finish scope, I still need [only the missing dimensions/location/desired appearance]. This completed [Brookswood/West Vancouver] example may help explain the type of result: [matching project link]. Once those details are clear, we can decide whether a call, further photographs or a site review is the useful next step.

Use Brookswood for an exposed-brick reference and West Vancouver for a dark-tile reference. Do not tell the customer that their surface will achieve the same result before assessment.

## 3. Early research or unfamiliar material

Hi [first name], you can start with the appearance you want rather than a material name. Is the goal a softer texture, a lighter fireplace or a more pronounced feature surface? A photograph of your room and one reference you like will help us discuss suitable options. Have you decided approximately when you would like the work considered?

Do not equate an early-stage inquiry with low value. Record timing and a mutually appropriate follow-up rather than applying pressure.

## 4. Fireplace-specific uncertainty

Hi [first name], I would like to understand the fireplace and existing surface before suggesting a decorative finish. Please send a full view, closer photographs of the surround/edges and any available fireplace model or installation information. Existing construction and clearance requirements can affect the scope. I cannot confirm suitability or a final price from the photograph alone.

Do not provide a heat rating, approval of clearances or promise that rebuilding will be unnecessary. Appliance/gas/structural issues remain with the appropriate qualified trade.

## 5. Designer or contractor inquiry

Hi [first name], thank you for getting in touch about [project]. Please send the relevant elevations or drawings, location, approximate surface area, current substrate information, finish references and target construction schedule. Let me know which surfaces are within our proposed decorative-finishing scope and who is coordinating the adjacent work. I can review that information before we discuss availability, samples where appropriate and an estimate.

Do not guarantee sample production, a reserved start date, insurance limits or other credentials without checking the real business arrangements.

## 6. Unsupported application

Hi [first name], thank you for checking. Stile di Leo is not currently taking on [specific unsupported application]. Our current work focuses on selected interior decorative finishes, including fireplace transformations and feature walls. I would not want to recommend a finish system we have not confirmed for your application.

Do not turn a shower, countertop, Tadelakt or exterior repair inquiry into a sellable service through wording. Do not invent a referral partner. If recommending someone known, confirm their actual capability and availability separately.

## 7. Request for a price before enough information exists

Hi [first name], the main cost factors are preparation, surface area, edges/detailing, access and the selected finish. Could you send [missing items]? I can then explain what is included and whether a preliminary planning range or a site review is appropriate. A final estimate needs a clear scope.

Use actual approved pricing only. Do not insert a blanket minimum, cost-per-square-foot figure, discount or free-quote promise from a generic template.

## 8. Site-review / consultation handoff

Hi [first name], based on the information so far, the next useful step is [actual next step]. I would like to confirm [specific surface/scope questions]. If a site visit is appropriate, I can offer [two real available times]. Before booking, I will confirm the visit scope and any applicable charge with you.

Record the agreed appointment and preparation needed. Do not promise a free visit or claim a date is reserved before agreement.

## 9. Estimate structure and cover message

Use this structure with the business's actual terms. This is a scope checklist, not a new legal contract.

1. Project and customer reference; location; estimate date/version.
2. Specific surfaces included, measurements/assumptions and existing condition observed.
3. Protection and preparation included in the finish scope.
4. Proposed product/finish, colour, texture and sheen selection process.
5. Edges, corners, hearth/mantel/other details included or excluded.
6. Adjacent appliance, electrical, structural and other trade responsibilities.
7. Total/line items, applicable taxes and clear allowances; actual business pricing only.
8. Access, curing/protection and timing assumptions supported by the chosen system and real schedule.
9. Actual payment, variation, validity, acceptance and warranty terms approved by the owner.
10. Clear next action and a direct way to ask questions.

Cover message:

Hi [first name], attached is estimate [reference/version] for [specific surface and finish scope]. It sets out the preparation, decorative finish, assumptions and exclusions so you can see what is included. Please let me know if you would like to discuss any part of the scope. The proposed next step is [real next action]. Any start date is subject to our confirmed schedule and agreed project requirements.

## 10. Missing-details follow-up

After approximately 2-3 working days without a reply, if appropriate:

Hi [first name], checking whether you are still considering the [surface] project. I am missing [one or two specific details] before I can suggest the next step. If your timing has changed, let me know and I can update my notes.

If there is still no reply after one further check around a week later, leave the conversation open without repeated messages. Stop immediately on a decline or request not to follow up.

## 11. Estimate follow-up

Around 3-5 working days after sending, if no response and no different date was agreed:

Hi [first name], did you receive estimate [reference]? I am happy to clarify the preparation, finish or other items included. Is there any part of the scope you would like to discuss before deciding?

Optional later close-out when appropriate:

Hi [first name], a final check-in on the [project] estimate. Is the project moving ahead, postponed or no longer needed? I can update the file accordingly. If you return to it later, we can review the current scope and availability then.

Do not use invented scarcity, a fabricated deadline, an unapproved discount or repeated pressure. Confirm the customer's next review date where possible instead of imposing a generic cadence.

## 12. Recording and weekly review

Stages: inquiry -> qualified -> consultation/site visit -> estimate sent -> won/lost/pending.

Qualified means a supported service, workable location and enough scope to continue; it does not mean won. Record source, relevant campaign/content ID, application, city, next step/date, estimate value if sent, status and reason. Use existing register columns and private notes for the next action; do not create a duplicate CRM in these files.

Do not label every Google-origin inquiry GBP or every button click a lead. Ask "How did you find us?" only where useful and keep unknown attribution unknown. Match later decisions to the original inquiry ID to avoid double-counting reopened conversations.

After a completed project, request feedback/review honestly and obtain permission before publishing new photos or a testimonial. Use the neutral review template in the operating pack.
