Choose the Surface Before the Finish

One wall may be enough. Consider what you see from the sofa, the entrance or the dining table, then look at the nearby wood, stone and cabinetry. This helps establish whether the plaster should be a quiet backdrop or the room's focal point.

Texture and sheen should be considered in the room's actual light. Photographs taken in daylight and with the evening lights on help us discuss the effect you want without relying on a small screen reference alone.

Fireplace Walls and Statement Surfaces

An existing fireplace can be a candidate for a decorative plaster finish, but resurfacing is not suitable for every installation. Substrate condition, the fireplace system and required clearances need to be assessed. Include the opening, hearth and mantel in your photographs so the initial discussion covers more than the front face of the wall.

Explore fireplace finish options or planning a feature wall for the different applications. If you are comparing decorative plaster with ordinary painted drywall, our plaster and drywall guide explains the scope of the upgrade.

A Completed Fireplace in Brookswood, Langley

Our Brookswood project transformed a dated brick fireplace with San Marco Marmorino Antico. The preparation included mesh, base coat, primer and levelling before the decorative finish. The completed surface is lighter, with raised botanical detailing above the mantel.

The project took approximately seven days. That is a record of this fireplace, rather than a standard timeline for every wall: surface condition, detailing and drying stages affect the schedule. See the Brookswood fireplace before-and-after photographs and preparation details.

Planning Your Langley Wall

Send a photograph of the full wall, its approximate width and height, and a close-up of the existing finish. For a fireplace, include the hearth, mantel and adjoining surfaces. This helps us assess preparation and transitions before discussing the decorative finish.

For tall entry or stair walls, include access conditions and ceiling height. For an occupied family room, tell us which furniture can be moved and when the room needs to be available. We can then discuss a practical scope and sequence for your home.

Cost and Planning

Wall size, access, substrate preparation and finish complexity determine the scope. Corners, fireplace returns and built-in elements can add work that is not captured by wall area alone. Frequently Asked Questions

Can decorative plaster be included in a new build?
Yes. Discuss the wall locations, lighting and adjoining finishes before decorating begins so preparation and the construction schedule can be coordinated.

What should I send for an initial estimate?
Send full-wall photographs, approximate dimensions, your location and a reference for the look you want. Mention damaged surfaces, access at height and any work planned around the wall.

Can you resurface a brick fireplace like the Brookswood project?
That completed project shows one approach, not a promise that every brick fireplace can be treated the same way. Your substrate, fireplace system, condition and clearances need an individual assessment.
