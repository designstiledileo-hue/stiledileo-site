Project Story

From dark tile to a quieter architectural surface

The existing fireplace was finished in dark large-format tile. The completed project changed that surface into a lighter, more continuous Marmorino fireplace finish.

The finished fireplace now reads as a quieter architectural surface within the room, with subtle hand-applied texture visible under directional light.

What changed

  • The visual weight shifted from dark tile to a lighter mineral plaster surface.
  • The fireplace reads as one continuous feature rather than a grid of tile joints.
  • The finish catches directional light and reveals subtle hand-applied texture.

Material Context

Why Marmorino worked here

Marmorino was suitable for this project because the design objective was not to create a loud effect. The goal was a lighter, more continuous fireplace surface with soft mineral variation and a refined hand-finished character.

For homeowners comparing finish systems, the broader Marmorino Vancouver page explains where this decorative plaster direction fits within fireplace walls and feature surfaces.

If your project starts with brick, tile, or another existing fireplace surface, the correct approach depends on the substrate, fireplace system, clearances, construction condition, and scope. The surface should be reviewed before a finish direction is confirmed.
