# STILE DI LEO — GROWTH-005 QUALIFIED LEAD GENERATION ENGINE v1.0

## 1. Executive Strategy

Stile di Leo should pursue a focused qualified-lead engine, not a broad marketing machine.

Primary commercial wedge:

- Fireplace Transformations

Supporting services:

- Venetian plaster
- Marmorino
- feature walls
- decorative plaster
- microcement
- marble-look finishes

Target operating model:

- 10–13 qualified leads/month as a target range, not a forecast
- 4–5 closed projects/month as a possible operating objective when lead quality, project value, capacity, and market conditions support it
- 2–4 focused growth hours/week
- one-row lead and prospect registers
- project proof reused across search, GBP, B2B outreach, referrals, social, and sales conversations

The engine should optimize for qualified leads and won project value, not raw inquiries, followers, impressions, or generic traffic.

## 3. Fireplace Wedge Strategy

Fireplace Transformations should lead the acquisition message because they are easy for customers to understand.

Why it works:

- an outdated fireplace is a visible problem;
- the before/after transformation is obvious;
- the project scope is concrete enough for a homeowner to send a photo;
- fireplace work naturally demonstrates plaster skill, surface preparation, texture, and finish quality;
- one fireplace inquiry can lead to broader decorative plaster or feature-wall work.

Use fireplace-first positioning when:

- speaking to homeowners with dated brick/tile/stone/fireplace walls;
- posting before/after proof;
- approaching fireplace contractors;
- creating GBP project updates;
- discussing Marmorino over brick or dated surfaces.

Use broader decorative plaster positioning when:

- speaking with designers/builders;
- a project includes multiple rooms/surfaces;
- the buyer is exploring finish systems, not one fireplace problem;
- the inquiry is about Venetian plaster, Marmorino, feature walls, or microcement specifically.

## 5. B2B Prospect Criteria

Contact firms that show:

- residential work;
- custom homes or higher-end renovations;
- fireplace, feature-wall, or refined interior projects;
- Metro Vancouver / Lower Mainland service area;
- design quality where decorative plaster logically fits;
- evidence of active projects and professional presentation.

Avoid:

- irrelevant businesses;
- low-quality mass directories;
- pure commercial firms with no residential fit;
- firms clearly outside the service geography;
- companies where the service fit is forced.

## 6. Designer / Builder Outreach System

Weekly target:

- 10–15 high-quality new contacts/week
- plus follow-ups from prior weeks

Goal:

relationship → relevant project → repeat referral source

Not the goal:

“Please send me work.”

Simple workflow:

1. find firms with visible fit;
2. add them to the prospect register;
3. write one specific reason for fit;
4. send a concise first message;
5. follow up twice only if no response;
6. record response/status;
7. when there is interest, offer portfolio, samples, or project review.

## 9. GBP Growth Engine

1. upload real photos;
2. publish real project proof where appropriate;
3. request an honest customer review;
4. respond to reviews;
5. record review requested/received in the lead or project register.

Priority GBP proof:

1. fireplace transformations
2. strong before/after

## 10. Review Engine

Review objective:

- request a review from suitable completed customers, not an arbitrary monthly quota unrelated to project volume.

Track:

- review requested
- review received
- review date

Simple request:

“Thank you again for trusting me with the project. If you’re happy with the work, an honest Google review would really help future homeowners understand what it’s like to work with Stile di Leo. No pressure — only if you feel comfortable.”

Do not incentivize positive reviews.

## 18. Inbound Lead Response System

Respond quickly when reasonably possible.

First-response template:

“Thanks for reaching out. Please send a photo of the fireplace/wall, approximate dimensions, your city, and any finish or reference image you have in mind. From there I can tell whether it looks like a fit and what the next step would be.”

Do not require a long questionnaire before speaking to the customer.

## 19. Lead Qualification

Use the GROWTH-003 framework.

Qualified lead generally requires:

- service fit;
- location fit;
- real project;
- sufficient intent/context to continue discussion.

Response path:

GOOD FIT:

- continue conversation;
- request missing photos/dimensions if needed;
- move toward consultation/site visit or quote path.

UNCLEAR:

- ask minimal questions:
  - project photo
  - city
  - approximate dimensions
  - desired finish/reference

BAD FIT:

- politely decline or redirect.

## 20. Sales Handoff

Basic sales path:

INQUIRY
→ PHOTO / PROJECT REVIEW
→ QUALIFIED
→ ROUGH DIRECTION / RANGE IF APPROPRIATE
→ SITE VISIT / CONSULTATION IF NEEDED
→ QUOTE
→ FOLLOW-UP
→ WON / LOST

Do not redesign pricing during this sprint.

## 21. Quote Follow-Up

2–4 days after quote:

“Hi [Name], just checking whether you had any questions about the quote or finish direction. Happy to clarify anything if helpful.”

7–10 days later:

“Hi [Name], one final follow-up from me on the fireplace/plaster quote. If the timing changes or you’d like to move forward later, feel free to reach out anytime.”

Then stop unless the customer re-engages.

Keep it calm and low-pressure.

## 23. Scale / Kill Rules

Do not kill a channel after:

- one lead;
- one week;
- one failed outreach batch;
- one low-value inquiry.

Scale a channel when it demonstrates:

- repeatable qualified leads;
- acceptable effort/cost;
- commercially useful projects;
- evidence of quote or won-project progression.

Deprioritize a channel when it repeatedly produces:

- poor-fit inquiries;
- low-value leads;
- high effort;
- no meaningful opportunities;
- weak source-to-revenue connection.
